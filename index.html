Nine seed prompts — one per assumption

The four moves work on every card from the menu. Pick the assumption that made your jaw tighten; start with its prompt tonight.

№ 01 · Learning = letters & numbers

I want assessments that tell me what students actually know — not what they can perform under pressure. Help me rethink how I gather evidence of learning in my subject / grade. Start by asking me what I really want to know about my students that my current assessments aren't telling me. Keep everything grounded in modern research, not hype.

№ 02 · Content over transferable skills

I teach subject / grade. Take my unit on topic and help me redesign it so the content becomes the vehicle for one transferable skill my students will still be using in ten years — arguing from evidence, collaborating, revising their own work. Ask me first which of those skills my students most lack. The skill work has to replace existing busywork, not stack on top of it.

№ 03 · The teacher is the sole expert

I teach subject / grade, and my students' questions regularly outrun what I know. Help me make "I don't know — let's find out" a plan instead of a dodge. Ask me about my kids and my comfort level first. Then help me design a 15-minute inquiry routine we can run any day a great question lands — one where I facilitate the finding-out instead of supplying the answer.

№ 04 · Coverage over depth

I teach subject / grade under a pacing guide. Take my unit on topic and help me decide what to teach deeply and what to compress — without abandoning the standards. Start by asking me which standards actually recur later and which ones my students never truly get. Then help me build one deep week I could defend to anyone who asks.

№ 05 · Age = ability

I teach grade, and my students' readiness in subject spans years in both directions. Help me design one unit where students enter at different points and every one of them moves — without me writing three separate lesson plans. Ask me first how I currently figure out where each kid actually is. Keep it sustainable for one human being.

№ 06 · Time is the constant; mastery is the variable

In my classroom right now, time is the constant and mastery is the variable. I want to flip one unit: everyone masters it, and the time it takes is what varies. Ask me about my schedule constraints and about my fastest and slowest learners first. Then help me design the checkpoints, the re-dos, and what early finishers do that isn't more worksheets.

№ 07 · The bell owns the learning

I get X minutes per period. Help me redesign one recurring lesson so the thinking survives the bell — questions and threads that carry across days instead of resetting every period. Ask me first what my students lose most when the bell cuts us off. Small moves only: I don't control the schedule, and I'm not pretending I do.

№ 08 · Compliance before learning

I want to find out what my classroom actually rewards more: compliance or curiosity. Interview me — one question at a time — about what gets praised, what gets corrected, and what my quietest student did last week. Then help me redesign one daily routine so curiosity gets the attention that compliance gets now.

№ 09 · Tests measure schools

Our building's story gets told in test scores. Help me build a fuller evidence portfolio of what my students can actually do — artifacts, performances, growth over time — that I can put right next to those scores. Ask me first who the audience is: parents, my principal, or the students themselves. Then help me design something I can maintain in ten minutes a week.

Save it as a Gem — and the Arts Integration Coach

A Gem is just instructions + your files, saved — a colleague you set up once who's on call every planning period. In Gemini: Gems → New Gem → paste your prompt as the instructions → upload your files.

As seen live · Arts Integration Coach Gem — the instructions

I'm not artistic. Turn my regular lessons into simple arts-based activities using materials I already have. Ask me about my grade level, my space, and my supply budget first. Keep every activity 15–30 minutes, tie it to my lesson's learning goal, and cite the state arts standards I've uploaded.

The seatbelt: upload your state arts standards PDF as the Gem's file — that's who checks its work when there's no art teacher in the building. And to be blunt, the way we were on stage: the right fix is still hiring an art teacher. This is triage — the choice was never Gem vs. art teacher; it was Gem vs. zero art. If you are the art teacher: this is your student teacher, not your replacement.

That wasn't magic — it was four moves

1A role and a real constraint. Your subject, your kids, your building — specificity is the whole game.
2Make it ask YOU questions first. "Start by asking me…" flips who's interviewing whom.
3Demand grounding. "Research, not hype" — then actually follow a citation. You're allowed to check its work. You should.
4Save it as a Gem. Set it up once; it's there every planning period, forever.
